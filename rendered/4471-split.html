  • parser: a day-first tenant’s date comes back with day and month swapped (“8 January” shows as “1 August”). The shared parser reads every date month first. This is the bug #4471’s fix was for.
  • hint: the same swap, but only through the booking form of booking-v3 tenants. That form prints MM/DD/YYYY under the date field, so people type month first, and the form reads it day first. Fixing the parser doesn’t touch it. The fix is the hint.
  • oneDayMove: the date moved by one day (“Tuesday 6 October showed as Monday”, “Lisbon got Tuesday, Toronto got Monday”, “only June is one day off”). A parser that reads month first can only swap day and month; it can’t move a date by one day. This looks like a time zone or daylight-saving shift. The desk had already noted these reports (desk-notes.json), and #4471’s cause table counts them under parser, where they are filed by tenant. They are counted there too, and again as a problem of their own.

How they were counted

  • The export comes in overlapping pages, so one report can appear twice. Each report id counts once.
  • The cause comes from the tenant (locale and booking form) and the channel, as in causes.ts. The intake tag doesn’t decide it: it is a guess from the reporter’s browser.
  • Subjects don’t decide it either. The same subject (“Date is wrong”) covers more than one problem, which is why merging on subject (decision 0001) put them all in one ticket.

Lessons

  • Read what a report shows, not what it is called. A big ticket can hide more than one bug.
  • Closing a ticket closes the problems its fix covers, not every report merged into it.
